I'm the Proof The trilogy Suggest material

You keep describing yourself. I keep producing evidence.

Three songs, three opponents, one standard: when language meets reality, what remains standing?

Play the trilogy

Each song opens with an accusation, moves through behavior anyone could observe, and ends in a verdict. Every track has its own lyric display.

  1. You the Promise

    Words abandoned

    The deepest wound. A rival's broken promises changed what ordinary phrases mean, and the speaker stands in what was left behind. A promise points forward. Proof points back at what already happened.

    83.35 BPM. Expand, then cut: broad declarations build the case and short verdicts stop it cold.

    Open lyric display

  2. You the Talk

    Words that backfire

    The rival keeps warning her about the speaker, and every warning adds detail. Fear turns to curiosity. The warning becomes unpaid publicity, and the speaker never has to chase.

    86.13 BPM. Surge, then pivot: clauses spill forward like an argument happening live.

    Open lyric display

  3. You the Fan

    Attention exposed

    At a gas station, three men whisper while one woman watches. Their hate proves sustained attention. She crosses the space, asks who he is, hears the music, and judges for herself.

    86.13 BPM. Stomp, then freeze: a drill bounce, then a beatless bridge where silence cross-examines.

    Open lyric display

One escalation

The claim stays fixed while each opposing identity fails in a new way.

Promise

You failed to become your words. Proof means staying, rebuilding, and carrying consequences someone else left behind.

Talk

Your words now work for me. Each repetition strengthens the legend it was meant to destroy.

Fan

Your attention tells the truth. Watching, whispering, and repeating a name turns hate into involuntary promotion.

warning + repetition → curiosity
curiosity + restraint → backfire

The witness

The most important change across the three songs is the woman's role. In Promise she carries the damage. In Talk she tests the warning. In Fan she judges the scene herself.

She is not the prize at the end of a contest. She is the witness who makes every claim answerable to reality.

Not a demand to be believed. A record of what happens when talk meets consequence.
